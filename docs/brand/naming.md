**Not legal advice and not a clearance.** The conflict checks below are one or two web searches per name, run on 2026-11-14. Before any public launch the chosen name needs a proper trademark clearance (EU and Greece at least) and a check of the app stores. Nothing was registered, bought or signed up for. Domains are listed to check, not claimed as free.

## Why "healthapp" has to go

It is a placeholder, and it sits next to the Greek state's own app. A quick search for health apps in Greece returns the national **MyHealth app**, reported as launched in December 2025 with citizens' records in one app ([To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/society/myhealthapp-launches-full-digital-health-records-in-one-app)). A product called "healthapp" would be confused with it on day one.

## What the name has to do

Taken from the brief and the live copy:

- easy to say in Greek and in English, at most 3 syllables;
- no medical claim (nothing like "diagnose", "doctor AI", "cure");
- no hint of insurers;
- works as an app name under an icon and as a wordmark;
- fits a calm, human, light tone, and the product's idea: paper becomes one history, the patient decides who sees it, every answer shows its source.

## Round 1: the six candidates (not chosen)

### 1. Kartela

- **Say it:** kar-TE-la (καρτέλα). 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** in Greek, "η καρτέλα" is the patient's chart a doctor keeps. Here the chart belongs to the patient.
- **Pros:** Greek patients and doctors understand it at once, with no explanation. Reads and sounds like a name in English. Describes the thing without a medical claim. Says where the product is from.
- **Cons:** it is an everyday Greek word (also a browser tab and a colour chart), so the clearance should look at how well it can be protected in Greece. Means nothing to an international patient until the tagline explains it. The K-spelling needs one line of explanation abroad.
- **Conflict check:** no health or medical app or company called Kartela found in two quick searches (English and Greek). The Greek results were other practice tools with other names, for example [MedMobile](https://old.sigmasoft.gr/?p=798).
- **Domains to check:** `kartela.com`, `kartela.health`, `kartela.gr`, `kartela.app`

### 2. Mazi

- **Say it:** ma-ZI (μαζί). 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** "together". All your exams together, and you and your doctor looking at the same record.
- **Pros:** short, warm, easy in both languages. Four letters make a strong wordmark and app icon. No medical claim at all.
- **Cons:** says nothing about records or history, so the tagline carries the meaning. A common Greek word, likely used by brands in other sectors (not checked). "Together" could be read as "shared with everyone", the opposite of "you choose who sees it".
- **Conflict check:** no health or medical app or company called Mazi found in two quick searches. Nearest names were different products, for example [Mazoapps](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/mazoapps/id6504671870).
- **Domains to check:** `mazi.com`, `mazi.health`, `mazi.gr`, `mazi.app`

### 3. Deltio

- **Say it:** del-TI-o (δελτίο). 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** a record card. Greeks know "δελτίο υγείας", the health booklet a child gets.
- **Pros:** familiar and trusted association in Greece. Looks clean as a wordmark. Easy for English speakers to read.
- **Cons:** in Greek it also means a news bulletin and an ID card, so it is less specific than Kartela. Sounds like a tech product ("delta", "-io") more than a human one. Carries an official, state-form feel.
- **Conflict check:** none found in health. One product with the same name in another sector: Deltio, a website monitoring tool, listed on [BetaList](https://betalist.com/startups/deltio).
- **Domains to check:** `deltio.com`, `deltio.health`, `deltio.gr`, `deltio.app`

### 4. Nima

- **Say it:** NI-ma (νήμα). 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** "thread". One thread running through every exam and every doctor.
- **Pros:** the best metaphor of the six for a history in date order. Very easy to say anywhere. Short wordmark.
- **Cons:** a real conflict in a nearby field (below). Also a common first name, which makes search and app-store discovery harder.
- **Conflict check:** **conflict found.** Nima is a food-allergen sensor company (gluten sensor with a companion app, acquired by Medline in 2020, later Nima Partners): [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/nima/), [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/6sensor-labs). Naima Health is a close-sounding US health company: [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/naima-health).
- **Domains to check:** `nima.com`, `nima.health`, `nima.gr`, `nima.app`

### 5. Ena

- **Say it:** E-na (ένα). 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** "one". One place, one history. It is the hero line in a single word.
- **Pros:** matches "Your medical history, in one place." Three letters, simple in every language.
- **Cons:** too short to search for or to own. Reads as a first name in English. A close name already exists in health (below).
- **Conflict check:** no "Ena Health" found, but a close one: [Enna Health - Symptom Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/enna-health-symptom-tracker/id1625714316) on the App Store.
- **Domains to check:** `ena.com`, `ena.health`, `ena.gr`, `ena.app`

### 6. Folio

- **Say it:** FO-li-o. 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** a folder of pages. The paper in the drawer, kept as one set.
- **Pros:** the paper-to-phone story is in the word. International, and understood by doctors and hospital staff abroad, the first buyer.
- **Cons:** not Greek, and a little formal for patients. A crowded word across many sectors. Close names already exist in health (below).
- **Conflict check:** no exact "Folio Health" found, but close ones: [Folia Health](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/folia-health/id1403862750) (symptom tracking) and a records app called MediFolio in the same search.
- **Domains to check:** `folio.com`, `folio.health`, `folio.gr`, `folio.app`

## Round 1 recommendation (superseded by round 2)

**Pick: Kartela.** Greek patients and doctors already use this word for a patient's chart, so the name explains the product before the tagline does, and no health product with the name turned up. It makes no medical claim, has nothing to do with insurers, and is easy to say in English.

**Runner-up: Mazi.** The warmest and shortest of the six and the easiest wordmark, with no conflict found. It loses because it needs the tagline to say what the product is.

Not recommended: **Nima** (conflict found), **Ena** and **Folio** (close names in health), **Deltio** (fine, but colder and less specific than Kartela).

How the pick reads with the live hero line, unchanged:

> **Kartela**
> Your medical history, in one place.

## Round 2: ten new words

**Why a second round.** The founder saw the six above and replied, 2026-11-14: "propose new words". None of the six, and no variant of them, appears below. The round 1 recommendation above is kept for the record and is no longer the proposal.

Same rules as round 1: at most 3 syllables, easy to say in Greek and English, no medical claim, no insurer, no "AI", works as a wordmark under an app icon. The ten are spread across four styles on purpose. Conflict check: one quick web search per name on 2026-11-14 for a health or medical app or company of that name. It is not a clearance. Domains were not checked; the coordinator does that.

### Coined words

#### 1. Istor

- **Say it:** IS-tor (Ίστορ). 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** cut from "ιστορικό", the word every Greek doctor uses for a patient's history, and from "history" in English. The hero line is inside the name.
- **Pro:** means the same thing to a Greek and an English ear, and nobody else is using it.
- **Con:** said fast in English it can sound like "iStore". A little cool and technical.
- **Conflict check:** none found in a quick search.

#### 2. Papyra

- **Say it:** pa-PY-ra (Παπύρα). 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** from papyrus (πάπυρος), the first paper. Your paper, kept.
- **Pro:** the paper-to-phone story is in the word, and it sounds soft and human.
- **Con:** papyrus is ancient and dusty, the opposite of light and on your phone.
- **Conflict check:** no "Papyra" found. A close name: [Papyrus Health](https://papyruslanding.pages.dev), a clinical documentation product.

#### 3. Vivlo

- **Say it:** VIV-lo. 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** from "βιβλιάριο υγείας", the health booklet every Greek has had, shortened into a new word.
- **Pro:** short, lively, and the "viv" reads as life in English without claiming anything.
- **Con:** written in Greek, "Βίβλο" reads as "the Bible". The "vl" is awkward for English speakers.
- **Conflict check:** no "Vivlo" found. Close names: [Vivlio Health](https://village.ai/company/vivlio-health) (IT consulting) and [Vivoo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1436436525) (wellness tests).

### English words and compounds

#### 4. Onefold

- **Say it:** ONE-fold. 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** one, plus the fold of a folder and of a folded exam. Many papers, one fold.
- **Pro:** an international doctor or hospital buyer gets "one place" at first sight.
- **Con:** not Greek, and "-fold" is harder for a Greek speaker to say and spell.
- **Conflict check:** none found. Nearby names: [Twofold Health](https://help.trytwofold.com/en/articles/9792797-using-the-twofold-mobile-app) (clinical notes) and [OneLine Health](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6749094470).

#### 5. Daybook

- **Say it:** DAY-book. 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** a book where entries are kept day by day, in date order. That is how the history is shown.
- **Pro:** plain, calm, and it says "in date order" without a metaphor.
- **Con:** sounds like a diary the patient writes, and the product reads exams, it is not a journal. A diary app with this name exists outside health (from memory, not checked).
- **Conflict check:** none found in health in a quick search.

#### 6. Openbook

- **Say it:** O-pen-book. 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** "an open book", the same idiom in Greek ("ανοιχτό βιβλίο"). Nothing hidden: every answer shows its source, every access is listed.
- **Pro:** carries the trust promise (sources and the access log) in two everyday words.
- **Con:** "open" can read as open to everyone, the opposite of "you choose who sees it".
- **Conflict check:** none found. A nearby name: [HealthBook+](https://www.startuphealth.com/startup-health-blog/2023/05/24/healthbook-empowers-patients-with-personalized-health-data-and-predictive-analytics).

### Greek-rooted

#### 7. Selida

- **Say it:** se-LI-da (σελίδα). 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** "page". Each exam is a page, and every answer points to the page it came from.
- **Pro:** reads like a name in English (close to Selina) and ties to the product's strongest proof, the cited source.
- **Con:** an everyday Greek word (also a web page), and it does not say "one place" without the tagline.
- **Conflict check:** none found in a quick search.

#### 8. Diko

- **Say it:** di-KO (δικό). 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** "yours", as in "δικό σου". The history belongs to the patient.
- **Pro:** four letters, a strong icon wordmark, and the patient's control is the name.
- **Con:** means nothing in English, and in Cyprus DIKO is a political party (from memory, not checked).
- **Conflict check:** none found in a quick search.

### Wildcards: an object or a metaphor

#### 9. Tessera

- **Say it:** TE-se-ra. 3 syllables.
- **Idea:** a tessera is one tile of a mosaic: each exam is a tile, together they make the picture. In Latin a "tessera hospitalis" was the token a host and a guest each kept to recognise one another, which is the QR code between patient and doctor.
- **Pro:** the richest story of the ten, and it covers both halves of the product (one history, access by token).
- **Con:** to a Greek ear it is the number four ("τέσσερα"). A biotech company uses the name, Tessera Therapeutics (from memory, not checked).
- **Conflict check:** none found for a records app in a quick search.

#### 10. Keyring

- **Say it:** KEY-ring. 2 syllables.
- **Idea:** every key on one ring, and the ring is in your pocket. You let the doctor in.
- **Pro:** control and "one place" in a single object, and the icon draws itself.
- **Con:** says access, not history, and "keyring" already means a password store on phones and computers.
- **Conflict check:** none found in a quick search.

## Voice and tone

Five rules, taken from the copy that is live on the landing page (`docs/landing/copy.md`). They apply to the site and to every string in the product.

1. **Plain and concrete. Say what it does.**
   - Do: "Photograph your exams once." · "A doctor let in by QR, for a set time."
   - Don't: "an AI-powered health platform", "seamless", "revolutionary", jargon a patient would not use.
2. **Claim only what runs. Label the rest.**
   - Do: mark what is future as "Future" and what is invented as "Synthetic example".
   - Don't: "the only", "the first", "end-to-end encrypted", numbers we have not measured, testimonials we do not have.
3. **The patient is in control, and we talk to them as "you".**
   - Do: "You choose who sees it, and for how long." · "You see every access."
   - Don't: fear, urgency or guilt ("don't risk your health", "before it's too late").
4. **No medical judgement, ever.**
   - Do: describe the document. "Outside printed range." · "Not in this record." · "Read from your photo. Check it against the original."
   - Don't: "abnormal", "you may have", "possible causes", "diagnosis". No "AI" in copy for patients. The insurer line stays exactly as written: "Insurers never see records."
5. **Calm, short, human.**
   - Do: one idea per line, sentence case, everyday words, a full stop.
   - Don't: exclamation marks, emoji, all caps, hype words, long sentences that stack three claims.
